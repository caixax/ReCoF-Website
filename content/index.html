Cry of Fear 1.6a, rebuilt from its own source code

The original Cry of Fear with hundreds of bugs fixed, a much lighter renderer, smoother frames on high refresh rate monitors and Simon's story in co-op over Steam. The maps and the story haven't changed, and every speedrun trick still works. ReCoF installs into a copy of the game, so your Steam install is never touched.

What's changed

All of this is in the current release. If something changes how the game plays, there's an option to switch it back to the original.

Fixes

Fixed one bug at a time

Cry of Fear has plenty of crashes and softlocks that players just learned to live with. ReCoF has the game's source code, so each problem gets fixed in the code that causes it, and the bug list records how it was found and how it was tested.

  • Out of memory crashes at door transitions and after long sessions are fixed, along with the water effect freezing the game above 1920 x 1080 and the 18 second freeze after the intro videos.
  • Monsters don't walk through thin walls and closed doors anymore, and they turn at the same speed whatever your frame rate.
  • The final wheelchair scene doesn't softlock, and the original's save bugs are fixed. Your old saves still load.
  • If the game does crash, it writes a report instead of freezing on the last frame.

The balance fixes all switch back to the original with one option, rc_retail_gameplay 1.

Performance

Runs faster, looks the same

Cry of Fear's Paranoia renderer did most of its work on the CPU, one vertex and one surface at a time. ReCoF hands that work to the GPU and does it in batches. The game looks identical, and the frame rate goes up, especially on weaker PCs.

  • Models are skinned and lit on the GPU instead of one vertex at a time on the CPU.
  • Dynamic lights are shader-based, and one light anywhere on the map no longer makes the whole visible world draw twice.
  • The world is grouped by texture and drawn in batches. Rain, snow and grass go out in blocks too, and hidden faces are skipped.
  • Model bones are set up on several CPU cores in parallel.
  • Picking up an item or starting a music track doesn't stall the game anymore.
  • Laptops with two GPUs start the game on the fast one.

You can turn every performance option off. If something still stutters on your PC, the performance log helps track it down.

Performance options

Smooth frames

Frame extrapolation for high refresh rate monitors

Cry of Fear's logic runs 100 times a second, and the original drew one frame per tick. On a 144 or 240 Hz monitor that means the view moved in 10 ms steps. Smooth frames draws extra frames between those ticks, extrapolated from the last one. Your mouse turns the view right away, and the camera and your weapon move ahead at your speed until a wall or the floor stops them.

  • The game itself doesn't change. Logic, physics and the timing of your inputs stay at 100 ticks, so nothing a speedrun relies on is affected.
  • It runs as fast as your monitor, or at a rate you set, and spaces the frames evenly so they don't stutter.
  • Cutscenes, menus and the inventory keep one frame per tick, like the original.

It's experimental and off by default. Turn on Smooth frames in the launcher's options or type rc_smoothframes 1. With it on, cl_showfps shows both rates, like 300 fps (100 logic).

Story co-op

Simon's story with up to three friends

Play the whole campaign with 2 to 4 players, without Radmin VPN, Hamachi or port forwarding. Host from the Co-op lobby and invite your friends through Steam. They join over Steam's network, and nobody's IP address is shown.

  • Pick the campaign, the difficulty and the co-op rules in the lobby, then send Steam invites.
  • Proximity voice chat, so you hear teammates from where they're standing.
  • Saves cover the whole group, and each new level waits until everyone has loaded, so nobody misses a scene.
  • Cutscenes, phone calls, keys, teleports and the ending were written for one player. In co-op they work for the whole group.
  • Everyone plays as Simon, and respawning gives you back your weapons, your clips and your quick slots.

Everyone in a game needs the same ReCoF version.

Installing

You need Cry of Fear on Steam (it's free) and Windows 10 or 11.

Get the launcher

Download the ReCoF Launcher. There's an installer and a portable single file.

Let it make a copy

It finds your Cry of Fear, copies it to a folder you choose and installs ReCoF there. Every file it downloads is checked against a signed list.

Press Play

The launcher keeps ReCoF updated and backs up your saves before each update. If an update gives you trouble, you can roll back. Your Steam game stays exactly as it was.

If you're on Windows 11 with Smart App Control on, you'll need to turn it off first, since ReCoF's files aren't signed yet. The install guide shows how.

Servers

Host a co-op server

ReCoF Server is a dedicated server for Cry of Fear co-op, built on ReHLDS, that runs on Windows or a Linux VPS. It's still in testing, and the first release isn't out yet.

Hosting a server
  • Runs on Windows or Linux with no window, and the game doesn't need to be running.
  • Takes Metamod-R and AMX Mod X plugins, with builds made for Cry of Fear.
  • The map waits for players, so its music, cameras and scripts start when the first one joins.
  • ReCoF players only, unless you let everyone in.

How it's made

Rebuilt from the source

ReCoF starts from DeCoF, a complete decompilation of every Cry of Fear binary (the game code, the engine, the renderer, the menus and both launchers) turned back into source code that compiles to the original game. ReCoF fixes and improves that source. The game, the launcher, the server and its plugins are all open source on GitHub, and ReCoF's own code is under the MIT License.
