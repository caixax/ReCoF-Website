Fixes

Bugs fixed where they start

Cry of Fear crashes, freezes and misbehaves in ways its players learned to live with. With the game's code in hand, ReCoF fixes each problem in the code that causes it, not with a patch on top, and writes down how it was found and how it was tested.

  • Crashes and freezes: out of memory crashes at door transitions and after long sessions, the water effect above 1920 x 1080, an 18 second freeze after the intro videos.
  • AI and collision: monsters no longer step through thin walls and closed doors, nor turn faster or slower with the frame rate.
  • Saves and story: the final wheelchair scene no longer softlocks, and save problems of the original are fixed. Saves from the original game still load.
  • A crash leaves a report instead of freezing the game on its last picture.

The balance fixes go back to the original with one option, rc_retail_gameplay 1.

Performance

The same picture with far less work

The Paranoia renderer behind Cry of Fear did most of its work on the processor, one vertex and one surface at a time. ReCoF moves that work to the graphics card and does it in bulk, so the picture stays the same and the frame rate goes up, most of all on weaker PCs.

  • Models on the graphics card: skinned and lit by the GPU instead of one vertex at a time on the CPU.
  • Lights with a shader, and one dynamic light anywhere on the map no longer makes the visible world draw twice.
  • Batched drawing: the world grouped by texture, rain, snow and grass in batches, hidden faces skipped.
  • Several cores: the bones of every model set up in parallel.
  • Fewer stutters: picking up an item or starting a music track no longer stalls the game.
  • Laptops with two graphics chips start the game on the fast one.

Every performance option can be turned off, and a performance log helps find what still stutters on your PC.

Performance options

Smooth frames

Frame extrapolation for fast monitors

Cry of Fear's logic runs 100 times a second and drew one picture each time, so on a 144 or 240 Hz monitor the view moved in 10 ms steps. Smooth frames draws extra pictures between those ticks, extrapolated from the last one: the mouse turns the view at once, and the camera and your weapon move ahead by your speed, stopped by walls and floors.

  • The game itself does not change: logic, physics and the timing of your inputs stay at their 100 ticks, so nothing a speedrun relies on moves.
  • As fast as your monitor, or a rate you set, with the pictures spaced evenly so they do not stutter.
  • Scenes, menus and the inventory keep one picture per tick, as in the original.

Experimental and off by default: turn on Smooth frames in the launcher's options or type rc_smoothframes 1. With it on, cl_showfps shows both, as in 300 fps (100 logic).

Story co-op

Simon's story with friends, straight through Steam

Play the whole campaign with 2 to 4 players. No Radmin VPN, no Hamachi, no port forwarding: host from the Co-op lobby, invite your friends through Steam, and they join over Steam's network without anyone's IP address being shown.

  • A lobby before the game: pick the campaign, the difficulty and the co-op rules, then send Steam invites.
  • Proximity voice chat: a teammate sounds from where he stands, near or far.
  • Saves for the whole group, and every new level waits until everyone has loaded, so nobody misses a scene.
  • The story reaches everyone: scenes, phone calls, keys, teleports and the ending, written for one player, work for the group.
  • Everyone plays as Simon, and a respawn gives back your weapons, your clips and your quick slots.

Everyone in a game needs the same ReCoF version.

Install

Play in three steps

You need Cry of Fear on Steam (it is free) and Windows 10 or 11.

Get the launcher

Download the ReCoF Launcher: an installer, or one portable file.

Let it make a copy

It finds your Cry of Fear, copies it to a folder you choose and installs ReCoF on the copy, checking every file it downloads against a signed list.

Press Play

It keeps ReCoF up to date, backs up your saves before every update and can roll back. Your Steam game stays exactly as it was.

On Windows 11 with Smart App Control on, turn it off first: ReCoF's files are not signed yet. The install guide shows how.

Servers

Host a co-op server

ReCoF Server is a dedicated server for Cry of Fear co-op on Windows or a Linux VPS, built on ReHLDS. It is being tested; its first release is coming.

Hosting a server
  • Windows or Linux, without a window and without the game running.
  • Plugins through Metamod-R and AMX Mod X, built for Cry of Fear.
  • Waits for players: the map's music, cameras and scripts start for the first one in.
  • ReCoF players only, unless you let everyone in.

How it is made

Rebuilt from the source

ReCoF starts from DeCoF, a complete decompilation of every Cry of Fear binary (the game code, the engine, the renderer, the menus and both launchers) turned back into source code that compiles to the original game. ReCoF fixes and improves that source. Everything is open source on GitHub: the game, the launcher, the server and its plugins, with ReCoF's own code under the MIT License.
